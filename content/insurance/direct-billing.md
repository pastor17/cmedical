Direct billing means your insurer pays the hospital directly, so you pay little or nothing out of pocket. Here’s how it works.

## When it’s available
- Your insurer must have a **network agreement** with the hospital (or its international department).
- It’s more common with **large international insurers** and their global medical networks.
- **Not all hospitals** are in every insurer’s network — confirm specifically.

## How a guarantee letter works
If direct billing applies, your insurer issues a **guarantee letter** (a document confirming they’ll cover the cost). You present this at the hospital, and they bill your insurer directly.

## What to confirm with your insurer
- Whether your hospital is **in-network**
- Whether a **guarantee letter** will be issued, and how long it takes
- Any **deductibles, co-pays, or coverage limits**
- What you might still pay **out of pocket**

> ⚠️ **Direct billing is not universal.** If your insurer isn’t in-network with your hospital, you’ll use pay-and-reimburse instead.
