Booking a hospital appointment in China is very different from home: most top hospitals use **online booking platforms** or their own **WeChat app**, and the main domestic platforms are built for Chinese patients. Here is how it actually works, and the most reliable routes for an international patient.

## The main booking channels (and what they can do)

### 1. Hospital WeChat service account / app (most reliable for foreigners)
Nearly every top hospital runs a **WeChat public account (公众号)** or a dedicated **app**. This is usually the best route for you because:
- You can often **select a specific doctor** (not just a department), which the 114 phone line cannot do.
- Some international departments offer an **English interface or English-speaking desk**.
- You get the actual appointment code to show at check-in.

**Beijing examples:** search WeChat for the hospital, e.g. **京医通** (for PUMCH and many Beijing hospitals) or the hospital's own service account.
**Shanghai examples:** hospitals use the **Shanghai Health Cloud (上海健康云)** app or their own WeChat account.

> ⚠️ You need a **WeChat account with a Chinese phone number or a linked payment** to use most of these. If you don't have WeChat set up, ask your hospital's international desk to book for you — this is the simplest path.

### 2. 114 / 116114 (Beijing unified booking phone + website)
In Beijing, **114** (phone 010-114) and the website **114yygh.com** are a unified appointment platform covering many public hospitals.
- **Limitation:** via 114 you generally **cannot name a specific doctor** — you book into a department/slot.
- Good for general department appointments; less useful if you need a particular expert.

### 3. Shanghai platforms: 随申办 (Suishenban) + 上海健康云
In Shanghai, the **Suishenban** app and **Shanghai Health Cloud** app let you search a hospital by name and book. Hospitals also have their own WeChat accounts. By the end of 2025 most Shanghai public hospitals accept an **electronic medical record card (电子病历卡)** for check-in.

### 4. The international patient department (recommended for you)
For complex cases, the cleanest route is to **book through the hospital's International Medical Department (国际医疗部 / 国际医疗)**. It handles:
- Appointments (often with a specific doctor)
- Interpreters / translation
- Coordination and a single point of contact
- Higher-tier (international) fees that match the quality of service you expect

**Real example (PUMCH, Beijing):** PUMCH's International Medical Services (est. 1951) offers regular, evening, and **VIP special-appointment clinics**, with a dedicated international fee (historically around **¥1,375** for an international/VIP consult, suited to patients with international insurance). Its health-management and endoscopy centers are in the Dongdan campus, with a parallel unit at Xidan.

## What to have ready before you book
- A short **English summary** of your condition (see [Preparing for your trip](/cmedical/preparation/))
- Your **passport details** (hospitals use it for the medical record)
- Prior **imaging/reports** on a USB or in the cloud, with **English translations** where possible
- A **payment method** (see [Payment in China](/cmedical/payment-howto/))

## The simplest, most reliable path for a first visit
1. **Email the hospital's international department** (or us) with your case summary + dates you can come.
2. They confirm an appointment slot (often with a specific doctor) and send the fee.
3. **Pay a deposit** (see [Payment protection](/cmedical/payment-protection/)).
4. They give you a **booking confirmation** to show at check-in.
5. On the day, arrive early, go to the **international / VIP entrance** if one exists, and use the **interpreter** provided.

> ⚠️ **Verify before you travel.** Booking channels, fees, and app availability change. Confirm the current international-department contact and your exact appointment directly before you book.
