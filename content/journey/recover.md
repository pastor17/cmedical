Getting your records and planning follow-up is where the trip can “break” if you don’t plan for it — but it’s also where good communication pays off most.

## Get your records before you leave

Ask the hospital (ideally in writing, and in English if possible) for:
- **Discharge summary / 出院小结** — a summary of your stay, procedures, and advice.
- **Diagnosis & treatment report** — what was done and the results.
- **Lab & imaging results** — reports and the actual images (CD/DICOM).
- **Medication list & prescriptions** — for continuity and refills.
- **Invoice & itemized charges** — for insurance reimbursement.

> 💡 **Ask for English copies.** Most international departments can provide translated documents; some may require the hospital seal/stamp for them to be recognized abroad.

## Medication & refills

- Get enough medication for the period you need, or a prescription you can refill.
- Ask about **cross-border prescription** options — can you have medication shipped, or refilled remotely?
- Note the generic names of your medicines so you can source them at home if needed.

## Cross-border follow-up

1. **Schedule follow-up** before you fly home — a return visit date or a remote check-in.
2. **Remote follow-up** — some hospitals offer online follow-up for stable post-treatment patients.
3. **Hand off to your home doctor** — send the translated records and reports to your local physician so they can continue your care.
4. **Watch for red flags** — keep a list of warning signs to contact the team (or local emergency services) about.

## Visa considerations

- If your treatment or recovery extends beyond your visa’s permitted stay, ask about **staying extension** or the appropriate **medical (S2) visa**. Start this process early.

> ⚠️ **Don’t fly home without your records.** It’s far harder to retrieve documents after you’ve left the country.
