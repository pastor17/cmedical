Many patients travel with a family member, friend, or professional caregiver. It's a good idea for most procedures. Here's how to plan for an accompanying person.

## Why bring someone

- **Support** during a stressful process.
- **Translation backup** (if they speak some Chinese).
- **Help with logistics** — running errands, navigation.
- **Comfort and advocacy** at appointments and after surgery.
- **Essential** for surgery and any procedure with anesthesia.

## The companion's practicalities

- **Visa** — the companion needs their own visa (usually the same type as you). We can help with their documentation.
- **Accommodation** — they can stay with you (hotel twin/double, or apartment). We recommend options for two.
- **Meals & transport** — plan for two.
- **Cost** — the companion is not a patient; their treatment isn't billed, but their travel/stay are.

## The companion's role

- **At appointments:** take notes, ask questions, help with translation.
- **During recovery:** help with mobility, meals, and daily needs.
- **Advocacy:** help ensure you understand and consent to things.
- **Logistics:** manage bookings, transport, and errands.

> ⚠️ **Privacy** — the companion can be present with your consent. The hospital respects patient privacy.

## If the patient is a child

- A **parent or legal guardian** must accompany and consent.
- Bring the **child's medical records, vaccination card, and ID**.
- The hospital's **pediatric** or **international** team will guide you.

## Tips

- **Agree on roles** in advance — who drives, who takes notes, who handles money.
- **Don't let the companion take on medical decisions** — that's for you and the doctors.
- **Plan rest** for the companion too — they may be tired.
- **Share the itinerary** with a third party at home.
