Cupping (拔罐, bá guàn) is a popular TCM therapy where cups are placed on the skin to create suction. Here's what it involves.

## How it works
Cups (glass, bamboo, or plastic) are placed on the skin — often the back, shoulders, or limbs — to create **suction**. This is believed to move qi and blood, release tension, and promote healing.

## Types
- **Stationary cupping** — cups stay in place for several minutes.
- **Gliding cupping** — cups are moved over the skin with lubrication.
- **Wet cupping (刺络拔罐)** — small punctures before cupping (less common; done in specific cases).

## What to expect
- A relaxing, warm sensation.
- **Circular marks** (bruising) that fade over a few days to a week — this is normal.
- A typical session is 10–30 minutes.

## Practical tips
- Tell the practitioner about any **blood-thinners** or skin conditions.
- Avoid hot baths or heavy exercise right after.
- It's often used alongside **tuina (massage)** or acupuncture.

> ⚠️ Cupping is generally safe when done by a qualified practitioner, but has contraindications. Discuss your health history first.
