Traditional Chinese Medicine (TCM) is a system of health practice developed over thousands of years in China. It’s still widely used today, and in China it often works **alongside** modern (Western) medicine rather than replacing it. This is a neutral, plain-English introduction for readers who are new to it.

> ⚠️ **Important:** TCM is best understood as **complementary** to modern medicine. Serious or acute conditions should be diagnosed and managed with modern diagnostics and treatment. TCM is most commonly used for **chronic, functional, and rehabilitation** concerns.

## The big idea

Rather than treating a single symptom in isolation, TCM traditionally looks at the **whole person** — patterns of symptoms, lifestyle, and balance — and aims to restore harmony. Think of it as a different *lens* on health, not a competitor to modern medicine.

## Common practices

- **Acupuncture** — thin needles placed at specific points, often used for pain, nausea, and rehabilitation.
- **Herbal medicine** — formulas of plant, mineral, and animal-derived ingredients, individualized by a TCM practitioner.
- **Tuina** — therapeutic massage/manipulation for musculoskeletal tension and pain.
- **Cupping & moxibustion** — cupping (suction cups) and moxa (warming), used for circulation and certain pain types.
- **Dietary therapy & qigong** — food choices and gentle movement practices for health and recovery.

## Where it’s often used

- Chronic pain (back, neck, joints)
- Post-illness or post-surgery **rehabilitation**
- Digestive and functional complaints
- Stress, sleep, and general “wellness” maintenance

## What to keep in mind

1. **Get a modern diagnosis first** for anything serious or new.
2. **Talk to your main treating team** about TCM — some herbal products can interact with medications.
3. **Manage expectations** — for many chronic conditions, TCM is about *managing and reducing* symptoms over time, not an instant cure.
4. **Choose qualified practitioners** and reputable hospitals with TCM departments.

> 💡 TCM is one of the things that makes healthcare in China unique. If you’re open to it, it can be a valuable part of a combined care plan — approached with the right context and communication.
