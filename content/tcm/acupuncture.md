Acupuncture is one of the most recognized forms of Traditional Chinese Medicine. It involves placing very thin needles at specific points on the body. Here’s a plain-English overview for people who are considering it.

> ⚠️ **General information, not medical advice.** Acupuncture is usually used as a **complement** to modern care. Discuss it with your treating team, especially if you take medication or have a bleeding or immune condition.

## What it is

A practitioner inserts thin, sterile needles into specific **acupoints** on the body. The needles are left in place for a short period (typically 20–40 minutes). Most people describe it as mild pressure, tingling, or warmth rather than sharp pain.

## What it’s commonly used for

- **Pain** — back, neck, knee, headache, post-operative pain
- **Nausea** — including nausea associated with chemotherapy
- **Rehabilitation** — supporting recovery after illness or surgery
- **Stress and sleep** — as part of a broader plan

Evidence and experience vary by condition; acupuncture is most often considered for **chronic and functional** concerns rather than acute emergencies.

## What a session is like

1. Consultation and assessment.
2. Needles placed (sterile, single-use), then left for 20–40 minutes.
3. A course of sessions is typical (rather than one-off) for many conditions.

## Things to know

- It’s usually a **course of treatments**, not a single fix.
- **Qualified practitioners and reputable hospitals** matter — choose TCM departments at established institutions.
- Tell your practitioner about any **medications or conditions** (e.g., blood thinners).
