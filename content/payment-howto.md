Paying for care in China is easy in most places but has **hospital-specific quirks**. Here is the real, current picture.

## Good news: foreign cards now work on WeChat & Alipay (since July 2023)
In **July 2023**, the State Council pushed payment convenience for foreigners, and **WeChat Pay and Alipay fully opened binding of Visa, Mastercard, and other major foreign cards**. Since then:
- **Alipay:** small transactions can be used **without full verification** (小额免认证), and foreign-card transaction limits were raised.
- **WeChat Pay:** per-transaction and annual limits for foreign cards were raised.

So for everyday spending (taxi, food, shopping) you are fine with **Alipay/WeChat linked to your foreign card**.

## The catch at hospitals: not all windows take foreign cards
Even with the 2023 changes, **large (Grade III-A) hospitals and pharmacies have not all fully covered foreign-card acceptance**. In practice:
- The **international / VIP department** is the most likely to accept a **foreign card** or a card-linked WeChat/Alipay.
- Some **domestic** cashiers only take **RMB cash** or a **Chinese bank card**.
- **WeChat Pay / Alipay with a linked foreign card** is the most widely accepted route overall.

### What to do
1. **Before you go:** set up **Alipay and WeChat Pay** and **link your Visa/Mastercard** to both (test a small purchase).
2. **Carry some RMB cash** as a backup (¥2,000–5,000 is a sensible amount).
3. **Confirm with the hospital** whether your specific department takes your card or card-linked app, and whether a **deposit (押金)** is required on admission.
4. For larger bills, ask whether they can **split payment** or issue an **invoice (发票)** for your insurance claim.

## Deposits and large payments
- Most hospitals ask for a **deposit on admission**; the balance is settled at discharge after the final bill.
- Keep **all receipts and the official invoice (发票)** — you need them for insurance reimbursement (see [Insurance & claims](/cmedical/insurance-claims-howto/)).
- For big amounts, hospitals may prefer a **bank transfer to a hospital account** — get the account details **in writing from the hospital directly**.

## Avoiding problems
- **Never** pay into a personal bank account or scan a personal QR code for a large medical payment — pay through the **hospital's official cashier/app** only.
- Keep a **paper copy** of every receipt (some systems change; your copy protects you).
- If a window can't take your card, ask for the **international desk** — that's usually the card-friendly route.

> ⚠️ **Verify before you travel.** Payment rules change and vary by hospital and even by cashier window. Confirm your card/app works and the deposit policy directly before you book.
