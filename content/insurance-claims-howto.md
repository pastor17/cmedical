Getting your treatment reimbursed is mostly about **paperwork and timing**. Here's the real process.

## The two models
- **Direct billing (直付):** the hospital bills your insurer directly. Only some hospitals + some international insurers do this (e.g., certain centers that accept **MSH** and similar). It's the smoothest if available.
- **Reimbursement (报销):** you **pay first**, then claim from your insurer. This is the **most common** model for foreign patients in China.

Check with **both** your insurer and the hospital which model applies to you.

## The "report first" rule (报案)
Most policies require you to **notify your insurer (报案) before or shortly after** non-emergency treatment.
- **Plan ahead:** for planned treatment, notify your insurer **before** the procedure.
- **Emergency:** if you can't call in time, **treat first, then report** — policies explicitly allow this ("急诊来不及打电话，先去看病，之后再补打").

## Documents you need for a clean claim
Gather these **before you leave China** (the official invoice is especially hard to replace later):
- **Official invoice (发票)** + **itemized bill**
- **Discharge summary (出院小结)**
- **Diagnosis & procedure report**
- **All lab results + imaging** (USB/CD + print)
- **Operative report** (if surgery)
- **Medication list**
- **Proof of payment** (receipts / bank transfer confirmation)
- Your **passport** copy and **policy number**
- **English translations** of the key medical documents

## Filing the claim — a clean process
1. **Notify** your insurer (报案) — before or immediately after treatment.
2. **Pay** the hospital; collect the **发票 + itemized bill + all documents**.
3. **File** the claim within your policy's deadline (often 30–90 days) with the full document set.
4. **Follow up** — claims with incomplete documents are the #1 cause of delays. Keep copies of everything you submit.
5. **Check exclusions** — some policies exclude pre-existing conditions, elective procedures, or require a waiting period. Confirm **before** you book, not after.

## Common pitfalls
- **Missing the 发票** — get it at discharge; it's your primary claim document.
- **No translation** — insurers often need English copies of the key medical records.
- **Filing late** — most policies have a strict deadline.
- **Unlisted exclusions** — read your policy's exclusions and get written confirmation of what's covered **before** treatment.
