> **⚠️ In a medical emergency, call 120 (China's emergency number) immediately.** If you can't call, ask nearby people for help or have someone call. Do not wait.

## Emergency numbers

- **120** — Medical emergency (ambulance) — *the number to call first*
- **110** — Police (can help coordinate in an emergency)
- **119** — Fire (also assists in some emergencies)
- **Your embassy/consulate** — keep their number; they can help with urgent matters

> 💡 **Save these in your phone** before you travel, and keep a **printed copy** in your bag.

## What to do in an emergency

1. **Call 120** — describe the situation and your location. If English help is needed, also call your hotel front desk or our support line.
2. **Stay calm and stay with the patient** — keep them still if there's possible injury.
3. **Have your details ready** — name, passport number, address, and any known conditions/medications.
4. **Let emergency services lead** — they'll transport to the nearest appropriate hospital.

## Getting to a hospital (if not 120)

- **Ride-hailing (DiDi)** or **taxi** to the nearest Grade III-A hospital's emergency department.
- Major hospitals have **24-hour emergency departments (急诊)** — no appointment needed.
- Tell the driver "hospital" + the name, or show them the hospital address.

## At the emergency department

- **International patients** are seen in the emergency department; ask for the international service for English support.
- Bring **ID/passport**, and if possible, a **translated summary** of your medical history.
- Ask for an **English note** of the treatment given, for your records and insurance.

## Practical tips

- **Know your hospital** — if you're under treatment, confirm the nearest emergency department and how to reach it.
- **Keep a medical ID** — a card with your conditions, allergies, and medications (in Chinese, we can provide).
- **Keep your phone charged** and have offline maps of your area.
