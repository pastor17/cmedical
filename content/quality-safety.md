Cost is one factor; **quality and safety** are just as important. Here's how to judge whether a hospital and doctor in China are right for you — and what the signals mean.

## China's hospital tiers

Chinese hospitals are rated by the Ministry of Health on a tier system. Understanding it helps you choose:

| Tier | Meaning | What it means for you |
|------|---------|----------------------|
| **Grade III-A (三甲)** | The top tier — large, comprehensive, teaching & research hospitals | **Best for complex/specialist care.** Most international patients use these. |
| Grade III-B/C | Large, strong, but not the very top | Good general care |
| Grade II | Regional hospitals | Standard care |
| Grade I | Basic/primary care | Primary and routine |

> **For specialist treatment, choose a Grade III-A hospital** — the ones we profile on this site.

## Accreditation

- **JCI (Joint Commission International)** — an internationally recognized hospital accreditation. Some top Chinese hospitals are JCI-accredited; it's a strong quality signal.
- **National quality certifications** — China has its own quality systems.
- Ask the hospital directly about its **accreditations and quality certifications**.

## How to judge a hospital and doctor

When choosing, look for:

- **Volume** — how many of *your specific* procedure do they perform each year? Higher volume often means more refined technique.
- **Specialization** — is there a dedicated center/department for your condition (e.g., a heart center, an eye center)?
- **The doctor's credentials** — years of experience, case numbers for your procedure, training, publications.
- **Multidisciplinary teams (MDT)** — for complex cases, does the hospital review cases across specialties?
- **Modern equipment** — imaging, navigation, robotic surgery where relevant.
- **International department** — do they have experience treating foreign patients, with English support?
- **Accreditations** — JCI or equivalent.
- **Patient outcomes** — can they share success rates or outcomes for your procedure?

## Red flags to avoid

- A hospital/doctor **won't give a written, itemized quote**.
- **Pressure to book immediately** without a proper consultation.
- **No international department** or English support for a non-trivial case.
- **No willingness** to share credentials, volume, or accreditations.
- A price that's **suspiciously low** — it may exclude important items or signal a less-established provider.
- No **post-treatment follow-up** plan.
