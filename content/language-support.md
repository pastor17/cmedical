"Will the doctor understand me?" is one of the most common worries — and the good news is that top hospitals have **real solutions** for this.

## 1. The international department = your built-in bridge
The **International Medical Department (国际医疗部 / 国际医疗)** at top hospitals (PUMCH, Zhongshan Fudan, West China, Ruijin, etc.) is staffed to handle English speakers. It typically provides:
- An **English-speaking coordinator** as your single point of contact
- **Interpreters** at your consult
- Help with the paperwork, records, and payment

This is the **easiest and most reliable** route if your hospital has one — book through it and the language problem is largely solved.

## 2. One-on-one full-process translation (一对一全程翻译)
Many large hospitals and some specialized centers now offer **one-on-one full-process translation** for foreign patients — a translator who stays with you from check-in through the consult and any procedures. Some report serving patients from **20+ countries** this way. Ask whether this is available for your visit and whether there's a fee.

## 3. Dedicated nurse / medical escort (专职护士陪同)
For international or insured patients, some hospitals assign a **dedicated nurse** to **escort and coordinate** the whole visit (registration, tests, results, discharge). A few hospitals that accept **international insurance** (e.g., via MSH) have a **multilingual team** for exactly this.

## 4. The 'companion' (陪诊) service — a real, common option
In Chinese cities, a **companion service (陪诊)** is a well-established, paid service: a local assistant who handles the **whole day for you** — pre-booking, hospital navigation, waiting-room logistics, result collection, and **translation + escort**. For a foreigner unfamiliar with the system, this is often the smoothest option.
- You can arrange one **through the hospital's international desk**, through a **reputable medical-tourism service** (such as us), or via a vetted local provider.
- Expect to **pay a daily fee**; it's worth it if your case involves multiple visits/tests.

## 5. If you arrange your own help
- Use a **professional medical translator** (not a tourist interpreter) for consults — they know the medical terms.
- Bring a **written English case summary** and a **symptom/timeline list**; even with a translator, a written summary prevents errors.
- A **translation app** is fine for everyday questions, but **do not rely on it for medical decisions** — use a human interpreter for anything important.

## What to confirm before you go
- Does your hospital's international desk provide an **interpreter** for your consult?
- Is **one-on-one translation** or a **companion (陪诊)** available, and what does it cost?
- Will there be a **medical escort** if you have surgery or multiple tests?

> ⚠️ **Verify before you travel.** Translation/companion services and their fees vary by hospital and change. Confirm availability and cost directly before you book.
