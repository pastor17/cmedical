*Name changed for privacy. This is a representative patient story, not a guarantee of outcomes or cost.*

I’m 62, from Manchester, with severe osteoarthritis in my right knee. In the UK I was told to wait, and the pain was getting worse every month. I looked at options in China.

## Choosing the hospital

I shortlisted Grade III-A hospitals in Chengdu (a hub I’d already visited) and asked for quotes. I chose a center with a strong **orthopedic department** and an **international service**. They sent a written plan and cost estimate in English.

## The visit

- **Pre-op:** Consultation, bloods, and scans. The team reviewed my case in an **MDT meeting** — that reassured me.
- **Surgery:** A single-knee replacement. I was in hospital about a week.
- **Physiotherapy:** This was the part I’d underestimated. Daily physio in hospital made a real difference to how I moved when I left.
- **Follow-up:** I arranged a return visit a few weeks later, then continued physio back home.

## Cost

The total was a **fraction** of what I understood the procedure to cost in the UK, and it included the implant, hospital stay, and in-hospital physio. I paid via **WeChat Pay with my card**.

## What helped

- A **bilingual companion** during the consultation.
- **Physiotherapy taken seriously** — it’s where recovery is won.
- Getting **written records** in English to show my GP back home.

## What I’d tell others

Do the research on the *department*, not just the hospital name. Take physio seriously. And get your records in English for continuity at home.

I’m walking my grandchildren up and down the hill now. That’s what mattered.
