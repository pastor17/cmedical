*Name changed for privacy. This is a representative patient story, not a guarantee of outcomes.*

I’m 48, from Melbourne, with chronic lower-back pain that had flared for two years. I’d tried physio and medication with limited relief. I was curious about acupuncture and came to Beijing to see what a **combined TCM and modern approach** might do.

## What I experienced

- **Modern evaluation first:** I got proper imaging and a diagnosis from the orthopedic department — important so I knew what I was dealing with.
- **Acupuncture course:** A series of sessions aimed at pain and muscle tension. I’m a skeptic, but the sessions were consistent and the practitioners were careful.
- **Tuina (therapeutic massage):** Targeted work on the affected area, which I found genuinely relaxing and helpful.

## How it felt

I can’t claim it “cured” me — but after a few weeks of combined treatment, the **frequency and intensity of my flares dropped**, and I was sleeping better. I continued some self-care and occasional sessions.

## Things to know about TCM

- It’s **complementary**, not a replacement for modern diagnosis.
- **Expectation management** is key — TCM for chronic pain is often about managing and reducing symptoms, not a one-off fix.
- **Communication** helped: I asked what the aim of each treatment was, which kept things grounded.

## What I’d tell others

If you’re open to it, a combined approach can be worth exploring for chronic, non-emergency pain. Do the modern diagnostics first, treat TCM as a complement, and be patient with the process.

> ⚠️ This is a personal account. TCM outcomes vary by person and condition. Always discuss treatment with a qualified clinician.
