Going to another country for care raises a fair question: **"What are my rights, and what if something goes wrong?"** This page explains your rights as a patient in China and practical steps if you encounter a problem.

## Your basic rights as a patient

- **Informed consent** — you have the right to understand your diagnosis, the proposed treatment, the risks, alternatives, and costs **before** consenting. Ask questions until it's clear.
- **Privacy & confidentiality** — your medical information is protected under Chinese medical confidentiality rules.
- **Clear billing** — you have the right to an itemized bill and to understand what you're being charged for.
- **Quality care** — care should meet the standard of a qualified professional at the hospital.
- **Access to your records** — you can request copies of your medical records and reports.

## Informed consent — make it explicit

Before any procedure:
- Get the **diagnosis and treatment plan in writing** (in English if possible).
- Ask about **risks, benefits, and alternatives**.
- Ask about the **doctor's experience** with your specific procedure.
- Confirm the **costs** in writing.
- **You can decline** any treatment. No one should pressure you.

## If you have a problem or complication

Act promptly and calmly:

1. **Tell the medical team immediately.** Complications are best handled early — inform your doctor at once.
2. **Get a written account** of what happened and the plan.
3. **Request your records** (in English if possible) — imaging, reports, notes.
4. **Document everything** — dates, names, conversations, receipts.
5. **Contact us** — we can help coordinate communication, translation, and next steps.
6. **If it's an emergency**, call **120** (see our [Emergency guide](/cmedical/emergency/)).

## Complaints and redress

- **Hospital patient-relations / complaint office** — most Grade III-A hospitals have a patient-relations or complaints department. Raise your concern there in writing.
- **International department** — they often mediate issues for foreign patients.
- **Health administrative authorities** — for serious issues, the local health commission can be involved.
- **Mediation / legal routes** — for significant disputes, medical mediation or legal advice (often in English from an international firm) may apply.
- **Your insurance** — notify your insurer early if a claim or complication is involved.

> ⚠️ **Be realistic about redress.** Cross-border medical disputes can be complex, and legal remedies depend on the circumstances. The most effective approach is **prevention**: choose a high-quality provider (see [Quality & Safety](/cmedical/quality-safety/)), get clear written consent, and document everything.
