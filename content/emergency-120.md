If it's urgent, **120** is the number. Here is what to expect and how to protect yourself.

## The 120 number
- **120** is the **national emergency/ambulance number** (equivalent to 911). Call it from any phone, mobile or landline.
- Operators and dispatch are increasingly able to handle **basic English**, but it's safest to also **show someone your address in Chinese** (ask your hotel to write it for you).
- In hospitals, the **Emergency Department (急诊 / ER)** runs **24/7**.

## What happens in a Chinese ER
1. You (or 120) arrive at the **Emergency Department (急诊科)**.
2. Triage by **severity** — life-threatening cases are seen immediately.
3. Basic treatment, then either: **discharge with follow-up**, **admission**, or **transfer** to a specialist unit.
4. You'll pay a **deposit** for admission; the final bill is settled at discharge.

### Heart attack / stroke — the time is critical
For **chest pain, stroke symptoms, or severe injury**, do **not wait** — call **120** or get to the nearest ER immediately. Major hospitals have **integrated chest-pain / stroke centers** (胸痛中心 / 卒中中心) with a fast "door-to-treatment" pathway.

## Language in the ER
- ERs are fast and **not as English-friendly** as the international outpatient desk.
- **Bring a written card** with: your name, passport ID, **allergies**, **current medications**, **blood type**, and a **one-line summary** of what's wrong — in **English and, if possible, Chinese**.
- If you have a **companion/translator** or the hospital's **international desk** on call, use them.
- Ask for an **English-capable nurse** if available.

## Insurance & the "report first" rule
- Most travel/medical insurance requires you to **notify (报案) before or shortly after** non-emergency treatment. **In a true emergency, treat first, then report** — the policy explicitly allows this ("急诊来不及打电话，先去看病，之后再补打").
- **Keep every document**: ER admission form, all receipts, the **invoice (发票)**, diagnosis, and discharge summary. You need them for the claim.
- If you have **international insurance** accepted at the hospital, ask whether it can **bill directly** (some hospitals do; most are reimbursement-based).

## Before you travel (so this never catches you off guard)
- Save **120** and your **hotel's address in Chinese** in your phone.
- Tell your insurer you're coming and note the **emergency reporting number**.
- Keep a **medical summary card** (see [Preparing for your trip](/cmedical/preparation/)).

> ⚠️ **Verify before you travel.** Emergency procedures and insurance rules vary. Confirm your insurer's emergency line and the nearest top hospital's ER directly before you go.
